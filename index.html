Optical Engineering & Camera Positioning Guide

1. Lens Focal Length vs Field of View (FOV)

The field of view is governed by the physical dimensions of the image sensor ($W_{\text{sensor}}$, $H_{\text{sensor}}$) and the focal length of the optical lens ($f$):

HFOV = 2 × arctan( W_sensor / (2 × f) ) × (180° / π)
VFOV = 2 × arctan( H_sensor / (2 × f) ) × (180° / π)
f = W_sensor / ( 2 × tan( HFOV / 2 ) )

Key takeaway: Shorter focal lengths (e.g. 2.8mm, 4.0mm) yield wider scene coverage but lower pixel density at distance. Longer focal lengths (e.g. 12mm - 40mm telephoto) concentrate camera resolution into a tighter area, providing the high pixel density essential for facial identification and license plate recognition.

2. Sensor Formats & Crop Factor

Standard Surveillance Sensor Sizes:
• 1/1.8" (7.18 × 5.32 mm): High-end low-light & ANPR cameras (Bosch DINION 7100i). Superior sensitivity and larger field of view per mm of focal length.
• 1/2.8" (5.18 × 3.89 mm): Standard mainstream IP dome and bullet cameras.
• 1/3" (4.80 × 3.60 mm): Traditional legacy CCTV format.
A 4.0mm lens on a 1/1.8" sensor yields ~84° HFOV, whereas the same 4.0mm lens on a 1/2.8" sensor yields only ~66° HFOV due to crop factor.

1. Optimal Mounting Height (h)

Camera mounting height determines the balance between surveillance coverage, perspective distortion, and vulnerability to tampering:

• Pedestrian Gates & Facial Recognition: Mount at 2.5m to 3.5m. Keeping height low maintains a near-horizontal line-of-sight to capture facial features rather than the tops of hats or heads.
• Perimeter Fences & Roadways: Mount at 3.5m to 5.0m. Provides wide area overview while keeping cameras out of vandalism reach.
• High Mast / Pole Towers (6m - 12m): For expansive yard or parking lot overviews. Requires telephoto optics to inspect objects at ground level.

2. Downward Tilt Angle (α) & Foreshortening

Recommended Downward Tilt: 10° to 15° (Maximum: 25°)
Near Blind Spot Cutoff: D_near = h / tan( tilt + VFOV / 2 )
Far Ground Cutoff: D_far = h / tan( tilt - VFOV / 2 )
Warning: Severe Tilt Foreshortening (> 25°)
When a camera tilts steeply downward (> 25° - 30°):
1. Face Capture is Compromised: Cameras see the crown of the head rather than facial landmarks required by EN 62676-4 Identification.
2. Vehicle Distortion: Roofs and hoods dominate the frame, and license plates appear severely keystoned/skewed, degrading ANPR OCR engines.
3. Near Dead Zone: A large blind spot directly beneath the camera pole is created. Always design camera positions with 10% - 20% field overlap between consecutive cameras.

Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR / LPR) Engineering

Successful ANPR requires strict optical and geometric alignment:

Standard License Plate: 0.52m Wide × 0.15m High
Plate Pixel Width: W_plate_px = PPM × 0.52
Recommended OCR Pixel Width: ≥ 200 - 260 pixels (400 - 500 PPM)
Maximum Vertical Tilt: ≤ 20° - 25°
Maximum Horizontal Skew: ≤ 20°
ANPR Installation Checklist:
• Pixel Density: Aim for 400 to 500+ PPM across the vehicle lane. While basic OCR can function at 150-200 pixels across a plate in ideal static conditions, moving traffic requires 200+ pixels for 99%+ accuracy.
• Shutter Speed: Use high-speed electronic shutter (1/1000s to 1/2000s) to eliminate motion blur.
• Infrared (IR) Illumination: Standard vehicle plates are retroreflective. Integrated 850nm IR illuminators bounce directly back to the camera sensor, making plates glow brightly while filtering out headlight glare.
• Optical Skew: Ensure the horizontal approach angle is within ±15° of the lane trajectory.

EN 62676-4 Standard DORI Reference

• Identification (≥ 250 px/m): Enables positive identification of an individual beyond reasonable doubt in court.
• Recognition (≥ 125 px/m): Enables operators to recognize whether an individual has been seen before and identify vehicle make/model.
• Observation (≥ 63 px/m): Allows observation of general clothing, actions, and event progression.
• Detection (≥ 25 px/m): Alerts security operators that a human or vehicle is present in the monitored zone.
• Monitoring (< 25 px/m): General situational awareness only.

Creating & Measuring Custom Target Objects

Use the "Custom Dimensions" preset in the sidebar to define custom site assets (such as access control bollards, perimeter fences, cargo containers, or security gates):

Target Pixel Height (h_px) = ( Target_Height_m × f_px ) / Line_of_Sight_Distance
where f_px = ( Frame_Width_px / 2 ) / tan( HFOV / 2 )
